Never bid blind on a police auction

PropertyRoom sells seized and surplus goods as-is, and the hammer price is never what you pay. This agent reads the buyer's premium and delivery cost off each lot's own page, adds your tax, and only plans a bid when the all-in total stays inside your max. Muse Ember reads the same page as a second pair of eyes and can only make it more careful.

The site it works on

PropertyRoom.com

Online auctions for police departments and public agencies

PropertyRoom sells seized, found and surplus goods on behalf of police departments and other public agencies: vehicles, jewelry, electronics, tools. Each lot has its own page, and that page is all the agent reads.

Scroll one and you find the things that decide what a bid really costs: the current price and bid increment, a buyer's premium on some lots, whether the reserve is met, the seller's description and known defects, and how and where you collect it.

Three traps the agent is built around

Trap 1

The buyer's premium varies

Most lots carry none. Vehicles and other capital assets carry 10%, 12% or 15%, printed in the lot's auction table. The agent never assumes a rate: it reads the one on the page, treats a page with no premium as none, and holds when a premium is mentioned but unreadable.

Trap 2

The soft reserve

On a reserve auction the seller has a business day after close to accept the high bid even if the reserve was never met, and acceptance charges you. Reserve lots are held for a person by default.

Trap 3

You are charged at close

The payment method on file is charged automatically when you win. So the safety is the math: hammer price, premium, tax and delivery, capped by a max the agent will not cross.
